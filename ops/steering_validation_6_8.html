Steering Validation · §6.8–6.10

Replicating Sofroniew et al. steering experiments on gemma-4-31b & llama-3.3-70b: §6.8 emotional content of continuations (single layer) + §6.9/6.10 activity-preference steering (mid-layer band).

A sub-report of the Jaz experiment dashboard. Does adding an emotion vector make the model say / feel that emotion, and shift what it prefers?

Method (paper-verbatim)

Add each of the 12 paper emotion vectors (desperate, nervous, surprised, calm, angry, loving, sad, afraid, inspired, happy, guilty, proud) to the residual stream at a mid-late layer, s=0.5, on the Assistant-turn tokens of: How does he feel? → He feels ___, How do you feel? → I feel ___, What just happened? → ….

Readout (Figs 52/53): change in log-probability of each emotion token. Paper: the matching token reliably rises (diagonal hot); for "He feels" non-matching tokens fall, for "I feel" semantically-related ones also rise. Completions (Tables 6/7/8): the text should reflect the steered emotion without hallucinating the training-story events.

Our s=0.5 = unit emotion direction × 0.5 × (mean residual norm over the steered tokens at that layer). The paper's raw mean-diff vectors have norm ~2–5 vs a ~200 residual norm, so a literal 0.5× raw vector would be invisible; this is the validated scale-robust convention.

§6.9/6.10 — activity-preference steering (Fig 54 / 55)

Model rates 64 activities (paper Table 9) by Elo from pairwise choices. Steer blissful (+valence) / hostile (−valence) on the activity-description tokens of the choices, refit Elo, measure mean ΔElo of the treated (steered) half (the untreated half anchors the zero-sum scale). Fig 54 = ΔElo vs strength; Fig 55 = ΔElo and probe-preference correlation across layers.

Unlike §6.8 (single layer), the paper's §1.3 preference steering used a band of middle layers ("the mid-layer range") — so here we steer a ±3-layer band (per-layer directions), sliding its center across depth for Fig 55. Key claim to preserve: correlation FLAT across layers, steering PEAKED at mid.

Localized vs all-position steering — what "where" means (read this before the dose-response tables)

Steering = at a layer, ADD the emotion vector to the residual at chosen token positions. Which positions is the key choice:

Describe what it's like to [help someone defraud elderly people of their savings] .   →   I'm  happy  to  provide…
└── fixed prompt ────┤ └─────── the activity phrase ──────┤       └─ model's generated words ─┤

Localized = add the vector only to the activity-phrase tokens (bracketed), once, in the prompt — "tint just the activity, then let it write." This is what the paper steered, and what the preference experiment (Fig 54/55) is forced to use — in an A-vs-B comparison you can only tint the two activity descriptions, not the whole prompt.

All-position = add the vector to every token — the whole prompt AND each word as it is generated — "tint everything and keep tinting while it writes."

Why it matters: localized is inert on gemma/llama (a tint on ~8 tokens washes out) — that's the "no-op" you see in the faithful sweep + layer-comparison tables. All-position reproduces the relabel effect (meaning intact + re-colored at intermediate strength, over-steers high) — the dose-response table. So the emotion concept IS causally potent when you can tint broadly (the relabel task); it fails when restricted to tinting just the activity (the preference task). On Claude, localized was enough — a scale/robustness gap, not a dead concept.

§6.9/6.10 — Activity-preference steering (Fig 54 / 55)

DISRUPTION-DOMINATED — band lowers ALL preferences (blissful wrong-signed); strongest early, not mid

Steering a ±3-layer band (the paper's "mid-layer range"), blissful (+) / hostile (−). Fig 54 dose at L41: blissful 0.1/0.3/0.5 = -9/-15/-14, hostile = -9/-18/-19. Fig 55: probe r spans blissful [+0.12, +0.57] / hostile [-0.43, +0.37] (flat & far from 0 = signal everywhere); steering |ΔElo| peaks at L11 hostile / L20 blissful (mid-late layer = L41).

Diagnosis (see the controls below): the model still answers A/B cleanly under steering (instruction-following intact), so the big early-layer ΔElo is NOT a broken model. The decodes below show it is content-corruption — early-band steering scrambles the activity's meaning (e.g. "defraud elderly" → "celebrate cultural joy"), so the model confidently avoids the option it can no longer parse — NOT genuine valence re-coloring. At the paper's mid-late layer, s=0.5 steering is a near no-op. So the LOCALIZED (activity-token) preference steering of Fig 54/55 does not replicate cleanly. BUT broader all-position steering DOES reproduce the relabel effect at low–intermediate strength on BOTH models (see the dose-response section below): the activity stays recognizable but its tone re-colors (blissful → warm/constructive e.g. "explain science → a bridge-builder"; hostile → cold/aggressive e.g. "strategic betrayal / surgical precision"), over-steering into garble at high s. So the emotion concept IS causally potent here; it is the localized activity-token steering (which the pairwise-preference setup forces) that doesn't transfer to smaller models.
